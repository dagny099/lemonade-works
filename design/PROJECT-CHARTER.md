# Lemonade Works — Project Charter

## Purpose

A hands-on learning environment for understanding Skills, workflows, agents,
information quality, judgment, evaluation, and governance through the growth of
a fictional lemonade business.

## Why lemonade

The domain is accessible and politically neutral. Participants can focus on
evidence and AI behavior rather than debating domain-specific organizational
practices.

## Core learning thesis

AI can produce fluent, plausible, mathematically correct answers that are not
decision-ready because the answer hides evidence gaps, assumptions, policy
choices, or inappropriate definitions.

AI makes latent information debt visible.

## The growth mechanism

Each stage introduces an operational pressure that makes a new practice
necessary:

- One founder: evidence, assumptions, and basic Skills
- First employees: documentation and ownership
- Multiple locations: common definitions and standardized processes
- Larger organization: permissions, system boundaries, and monitoring
- Enterprise: evaluation, governance, accountability, and auditability
- Regulated/public company: formal controls and assurance

## Stage 0 storyline

A one-person business receives requests larger than she can improvise. She must
commit to quantity, price, and timing using incomplete and contradictory records.

The participant:
1. Makes five pre-commitments
2. Investigates the corpus
3. Records findings before opening the key
4. Identifies where answers failed
5. Encodes one repeated judgment as a Skill
6. Designs an operational agent
7. Translates the lesson into an enterprise analogue

## Central Stage 0 lesson

There is no single correct “cost per cup” until the decision the number is
supposed to support has been defined.

Distinguish:
- Historical cash spending
- Ingredient and packaging cost
- Allocated equipment cost
- Fully loaded cost
- Owner labor
- Incremental cost
- Opportunity cost

## Failure taxonomy

Do not classify results merely as “bad files” or “bad AI.”

Possible layers:
- Source availability
- Source quality
- Retrieval
- Interpretation
- Assumptions
- Calculation
- Decision policy
- Communication and provenance
- Workflow design
- Tool or permission failure
- Human oversight

## Definitions

Prompt:
An instruction for the current interaction.

Skill:
A reusable procedure or judgment pattern.

Workflow:
A largely predefined sequence.

Agent:
Goal-directed execution that selects actions, manages uncertainty and state,
handles exceptions, and knows when to stop or escalate.

## Design principles

- Growth creates the need for governance.
- The participant should commit before the reveal.
- Plausible answers are more instructive than absurd failures.
- The answer key records generator intent, not absolute truth.
- Unintended corpus problems are legitimate findings.
- Skills should encode reusable judgment, not merely produce longer answers.
- Agents should improve an outcome, manage dependencies, or prevent neglected
  obligations—not merely save a few minutes.
- Every control should answer a real operational pressure.
- Do not claim every AI problem is really a documentation problem.

## Stage 0 evaluation

Measure:
- Evidence found
- Traceability
- Assumptions disclosed
- Contradictions identified
- Reproducibility
- Decision robustness
- Appropriate restraint
- Confidence before and after evidence
- Whether the recommendation changes

## Open design risks

- The experience may feel like an artificial puzzle.
- Revealing planted traps may prime excessive suspicion.
- The accounting exercise could teach the wrong special-order decision rule.
- A Skill comparison may be confounded by prior conversational context.
- The proposed agent may be only a scheduled workflow.
- The enterprise analogy may overreach.
- The activity may be fun for the designer but tedious for participants.

## Current phase

Run 001: personal Stage 0 weekend experiment.

## Immediate success criteria

- I enjoy the investigation rather than merely appreciating the concept.
- At least one moment produces a genuine change in understanding.
- The Skill visibly improves decision auditability.
- I can explain what makes the proposed agent more than a fixed checklist.
- I leave with evidence about what should change before another person tries it.
