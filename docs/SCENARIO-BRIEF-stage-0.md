# Lemonade Works — Stage 0: The Founding
## Scenario Brief

> **Usage note.** This file is deliberately free of questions, traps, and
> teaching points. It exists so that a person or model can read it and
> independently generate questions a business advisor would need answered.
> If you add the trap design or the question set to this file, it stops
> working for that purpose. Keep those elsewhere.

---

## The business

**Maple Street Lemonade.** One person, six months in.

She sells lemonade from a stand outside her house on Maple Street, and rents a
booth at the farmers market on Saturdays. Revenue runs around $400 a week —
roughly 130 cups, split about 60/40 between the market and the stand. Cups are
$3, and there's a bundle price posted on a flyer she made in the spring.

She does everything: buying ingredients, making the product, setting prices,
working both locations, and keeping whatever books get kept. There are no
employees. There is no accountant. There is no system.

## How the business actually runs

Ingredients come from two suppliers she found early on and communicates with
almost entirely by text. Lemons and cups get bought weekly; sugar comes in
50 lb bags every five weeks or so. Prices have moved since she started and she
has not written the changes down anywhere except in those threads.

Production happens in her kitchen the night before. The recipe is a ratio she
worked out in the first month, corrected once, and now knows by heart.

Sales get recorded in a spreadsheet when she remembers, and in a notebook when
she doesn't. Expenses get transcribed from receipts in batches, when the pile
gets annoying enough.

The farmers market has a vendor packet with rules about setup time, booth fees,
permits, and signage. She read it once in March.

## What state the records are in

There is no filing system. Records exist as: a partly-maintained spreadsheet, a
stock count she did once, a folder of receipt photos, an inbox she treats as a
to-do list and mostly ignores, some texts, some handwritten notes, and a few
scraps of paper with things written on them that were important at the time.

Nothing is labeled. Nothing cross-references anything else. Several things
contradict each other, and she has never had reason to notice.

This is not unusual. This is what a working solo business looks like six months
in, when the person running it is the entire system and their memory is the
index.

## Where she is right now

The business works. She is not in trouble. But volume is growing, and a few
things have started to slip in ways she can feel but not name:

- She sometimes over-buys, sometimes runs out mid-Saturday
- She is not certain her price is right, and has never sat down to check
- Things arrive in her inbox that she means to handle and doesn't
- She has a vague sense that some days are much better than others, without
  knowing which or why

She has never had to explain the business to anyone. Nobody has ever asked her
to justify a number.

## What is about to change

She is starting to get requests that are larger than one person can improvise —
the kind that require committing in advance to a quantity, a price, and a date.
Answering one of those requires knowing things she has never had to know
precisely.

That is the pressure that ends Stage 0.
